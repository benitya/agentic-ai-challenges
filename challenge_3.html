CHALLENGE 03

Accountability

The Accountability Challenge

For a complex human–agent system, produce verifiable explanations that help people identify who contributed to an outcome and who is responsible for errors.

What is this challenge?

Agentic systems can involve multiple models, tools, people and decisions. When an outcome matters, it becomes important to understand what contributed to it and how that contribution can be evidenced.

This challenge explores structured attribution and explanations that can be examined rather than relying only on a narrative produced after the fact.

The central question

How can accountability for consequential agentic decisions be represented, traced and explained to both technical experts and the people affected by the decision?

Core outputs

What teams are expected to explore

01

Structured attribution

Represent who contributed to a consequential decision and how, using a structured provenance graph rather than only a narrative.

02

Two explanation registers

Present the same attribution for a domain expert who will audit it and a non-expert affected by the decision.

03

Method evidence

Explain how attribution was derived using approaches such as counterfactual replay, causal intervention, activation evidence or graph analysis.

Accountability pipeline

From agent episode to explanation

1. Trace the episodeCapture relevant actions, agents, inputs and outputs.
2. Build attributionRepresent contributions as structured claims.
3. Test the methodUse evidence or interventions to examine attribution.
4. Explain the outcomeRender evidence for expert and affected-person audiences.

Potential programme artefacts

What this challenge could leave behind

  • PROV-AGENT: a proposed W3C PROV profile for agentic decision traces.
  • An instrumented episode benchmark with known causal structure.
  • Evidence comparing explanations for experts and affected people.
  • A regulator-facing view of what an adequate explanation of a multi-agent decision could look like.
